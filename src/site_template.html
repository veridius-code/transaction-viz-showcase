Method

What counts as spending

Detection uses operating spend, which excludes scheduled payroll and rent. Those payments are known in advance, and including them would flag every month-end. Chart 2 includes everything.

Baseline

Each day is first adjusted for its weekday, because weekends run at about half of a weekday's volume. The baseline is the median of the previous {{WINDOW}} days. Its spread is the median absolute deviation × 1.4826 over the previous {{SPREAD_WINDOW}} days, a robust stand-in for a standard deviation that a few very large invoices cannot inflate. Weekends are noisier as well as lower, so each weekday's deviations are also put on a common scale. A day's robust z-score is how many spreads it sits above or below its baseline.

Cluster rule

A cluster is {{MIN_RUN}} or more consecutive days with robust z above {{Z}} for spending, or below −{{Z}} for inflows (a drought). Days inside a cluster are taken out of the baseline and the baseline is recalculated, so a cluster never hides itself by raising its own "normal". All of these settings live in one configuration object in src/analysis.py.

Comparing a cluster with normal periods

For a cluster of length L, every other L-day window in the year that does not touch a cluster gives a normal value: its average per day. The cluster is then described three ways. First, as a multiple of the typical (median) window. Second, by the share of normal windows it exceeds, which is a descriptive percentile and not a p-value. Third, with a 95% bootstrap interval for its own average per day ({{N_BOOT}} resamples of its days).

Does it work on data it wasn't tuned on?

The ledger was regenerated with {{ROB_SEEDS}} other random seeds: same planted anomalies, different noise. At default settings the detector found {{ROB_FOUND}} of {{ROB_TOTAL}} planted anomalies, {{ROB_EXACT}} of them with start and end dates within one day. It raised {{ROB_FALSE}} false clusters in total across those {{ROB_SEEDS}} simulated years. The published sample (seed {{SEED}}) recovers all three planted anomalies exactly, with no false clusters, and an automated test checks this.

Limitations

  • The data is synthetic. Real ledgers have quirks this one lacks, such as quarter-end effects, bank holidays, and reversals.
  • The baseline needs about four weeks of history, so the first weeks of the year are not scored.
  • Short clusters have few days to resample, so their bootstrap intervals are rough.
  • Windows overlap, so the normal periods are not independent of one another. This is another reason the percentiles are descriptive.
  • A flag means "look at this", not "something is wrong". The natural next step is to check the transactions in the flagged period.

Data cleaning log

The raw export is deliberately messy: mixed date formats, amounts stored as text, duplicated rows, missing categories, and inconsistent labels. Every fix is logged with a row count. The cleaned ledger matches the generator's ground truth exactly, and a test checks this.
