How it works

The same three steps run automatically every time the data is refreshed.

  1. Tidy the dataThe raw export is cleaned up: duplicates removed, dates and amounts standardised, missing categories filled in. Every fix is logged.
  2. Learn what "normal" looks likeFor every day, the previous four weeks set the normal level, allowing for quieter weekends and ignoring a few very large one-off invoices.
  3. Flag and double-checkTwo or more days in a row well outside the normal range are flagged. Each flagged period is then compared with every ordinary period of the same length.
Terms used in this report
Operating spend
Day-to-day spending: suppliers, contractors, equipment, travel, software and so on. Payroll and rent are left out because they are fixed and scheduled.
Normal range
The band a typical day falls in, based on the previous four weeks and adjusted for the day of the week.
Flagged period
Two or more consecutive days well above the normal range for spending, or well below it for money coming in.
"x typical"
How many times larger the flagged period's daily average is than a typical period of the same length.
95% interval
The likely range for the flagged period's true daily average, allowing for day-to-day noise.
"Higher than all N normal periods"
A plain description of where the period sits among ordinary periods. It is not a statistical significance test.
Technical method (for analysts)

Baseline

Each day is divided by its weekday factor. The baseline is the median of the previous {{WINDOW}} days. Its spread is the median absolute deviation × 1.4826 of the deviations from that baseline, over the previous {{SPREAD_WINDOW}} days. Weekends are noisier as well as lower, so each weekday's z-scores are rescaled to a common spread. Detection uses operating spend only (payroll and rent excluded).

Cluster rule

A cluster is {{MIN_RUN}} or more consecutive days with robust z above {{Z}} for spending, or below −{{Z}} for inflows. Days inside a cluster are removed and the baseline is recalculated until the clusters settle, so a cluster never inflates its own baseline. Isolated high days stay in, because removing them would make the spread too narrow. All settings live in DetectionConfig in src/analysis.py.

Window comparison

For a cluster of length L, every other L-day window that doesn't touch a cluster gives one reference value: its mean per day. The outputs are three: the cluster's mean as a multiple of the median reference window; the share of reference windows it exceeds, which is a descriptive empirical percentile and not a p-value; and a 95% bootstrap interval of the cluster's mean per day ({{N_BOOT}} resamples).

Robustness

The ledger was regenerated with {{ROB_SEEDS}} other random seeds: same planted anomalies, different noise. At default settings the detector found {{ROB_FOUND}} of {{ROB_TOTAL}} planted anomalies, {{ROB_EXACT}} of them with start and end dates within one day. It raised {{ROB_FALSE}} false clusters in total across those years. On the published sample it recovers all three exactly with no false clusters, and an automated test checks this.

Limitations

  • The data is synthetic. Real ledgers have quarter-ends, bank holidays, reversals and other quirks.
  • The baseline needs about four weeks of history, so early January is not scored.
  • Short clusters give rough bootstrap intervals.
  • Overlapping windows are not independent, which is another reason the percentiles are descriptive.
  • A flag means "look here", not "something is wrong".
