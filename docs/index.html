On a normal day the business spends about $10k on operations, and that level moved +14% between Q1 and Q4. Two periods break clearly away from that baseline: Mar 11-13 (3 days, $101k in total, 2.9x typical) and Aug 11-15 (5 days, $109k in total, 1.9x typical). Each was driven by one category (Equipment in March, Contractors in August), so each can be checked quickly against approvals or purchase orders.

What it means

Money in exceeded money out by $708k across 2025. The 12 negative weeks that line up with month-end payroll are expected and recover within days. Only 1 other week went negative (week of 13 Oct: -$42k), caused by the Oct 14-19 inflow drought. The spending bursts did not push any week below zero, but they cut those weeks' net inflow by at least 72% compared with a normal week.

How it works

The same three steps run automatically every time the data is refreshed.

  1. Tidy the dataThe raw export is cleaned up: duplicates removed, dates and amounts standardised, missing categories filled in. Every fix is logged.
  2. Learn what "normal" looks likeFor every day, the previous four weeks set the normal level, allowing for quieter weekends and ignoring a few very large one-off invoices.
  3. Flag and double-checkTwo or more days in a row well outside the normal range are flagged. Each flagged period is then compared with every ordinary period of the same length.
Terms used in this report
Operating spend
Day-to-day spending: suppliers, contractors, equipment, travel, software and so on. Payroll and rent are left out because they are fixed and scheduled.
Normal range
The band a typical day falls in, based on the previous four weeks and adjusted for the day of the week.
Flagged period
Two or more consecutive days well above the normal range for spending, or well below it for money coming in.
"x typical"
How many times larger the flagged period's daily average is than a typical period of the same length.
95% interval
The likely range for the flagged period's true daily average, allowing for day-to-day noise.
"Higher than all N normal periods"
A plain description of where the period sits among ordinary periods. It is not a statistical significance test.
Technical method (for analysts)

Baseline

Each day is divided by its weekday factor. The baseline is the median of the previous 28 days. Its spread is the median absolute deviation × 1.4826 of the deviations from that baseline, over the previous 56 days. Weekends are noisier as well as lower, so each weekday's z-scores are rescaled to a common spread. Detection uses operating spend only (payroll and rent excluded).

Cluster rule

A cluster is 2 or more consecutive days with robust z above 2.5 for spending, or below −2.5 for inflows. Days inside a cluster are removed and the baseline is recalculated until the clusters settle, so a cluster never inflates its own baseline. Isolated high days stay in, because removing them would make the spread too narrow. All settings live in DetectionConfig in src/analysis.py.

Window comparison

For a cluster of length L, every other L-day window that doesn't touch a cluster gives one reference value: its mean per day. The outputs are three: the cluster's mean as a multiple of the median reference window; the share of reference windows it exceeds, which is a descriptive empirical percentile and not a p-value; and a 95% bootstrap interval of the cluster's mean per day (5,000 resamples).

Robustness

The ledger was regenerated with 30 other random seeds: same planted anomalies, different noise. At default settings the detector found 89 of 90 planted anomalies, 80 of them with start and end dates within one day. It raised 5 false clusters in total across those years. On the published sample it recovers all three exactly with no false clusters, and an automated test checks this.

Limitations

  • The data is synthetic. Real ledgers have quarter-ends, bank holidays, reversals and other quirks.
  • The baseline needs about four weeks of history, so early January is not scored.
  • Short clusters give rough bootstrap intervals.
  • Overlapping windows are not independent, which is another reason the percentiles are descriptive.
  • A flag means "look here", not "something is wrong".

Data quality

The raw export had 11,230 rows. 220 were duplicates and were removed. 4,584 dates in non-standard formats, 4,418 amounts stored as text and 336 missing categories were fixed. Nothing else was dropped, and the cleaned data matches the generator's known answer exactly.
